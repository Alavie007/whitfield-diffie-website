ABOUT HIM

The man who taught
the world to keep secrets

Bailey Whitfield "Whit" Diffie was born on June 5, 1944 in Washington, D.C. From an early age he showed a remarkable aptitude for mathematics and abstract thinking, earning his Bachelor of Science in Mathematics from MIT in 1965.

“Privacy is the power to selectively reveal oneself to the world.”

After graduating, Diffie worked at MITRE Corporation and later at Stanford's Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, where he met Martin Hellman and became consumed by a single seemingly impossible question: how can two people communicate securely without ever meeting to share a key?

Classical cryptography assumed both parties needed a pre-shared secret — making secure communication impossibly impractical at scale. Diffie refused to accept this, and in 1976 he and Hellman published New Directions in Cryptography — one of the most important papers in the history of computer science. Whitfield Diffie is one of the most important figures in modern cryptography. Born on June 5, 1944, he became known for his groundbreaking work on public-key cryptography. Together with Martin Hellman, Diffie helped develop a new way for people to communicate securely without sharing a secret key beforehand. His work has had a major influence on internet security and the way we protect private information online today.

Today his work underpins every secure connection on the internet: HTTPS, SSH, TLS, Signal, and the entire infrastructure of digital trust. Diffie’s contribution continues to shape the digital world today. His ideas form the foundation of many secure systems used for online banking, private messaging, shopping, and everyday internet communication. His work shows how mathematics and innovation can solve real-world problems and leave a lasting impact on technology and society.
